Q1. What are some Pros and Cons of Agile development (vs non-Agile)? List 2 of each. 

Ans. 

Pros of Agile: 
- Higher Flexibility: Teams can adapt to changing requirements quickly because project work happens in short, iterative cycles rather than rigid, pre-planned phases.
- Faster Value Delivery: Working software or usable features are released to users at the end of every sprint, rather than waiting until the entire project is completed.

Cons of Agile: 
- Scope creep risk: Because requirements are constantly evolving and flexible, projects can easily expand beyond their original boundaries if not strictly managed.
- Less predictable timelines: The lack of a fixed, long-term blueprint makes it difficult to predict the exact final completion date and total cost from the very beginning.

Q2. Explain the role and responsibilities of the Scrum Master vs the Product Owner. 

Ans. 

The Product Owner decides what to build to maximize business value, while the Scrum Master optimizes how the team delivers by removing obstacles and coaching Agile practices.

Product Owner's Core Responsibilities: 
- Product Vision: Defines the long-term goals and the product roadmap.
- Backlog Management: Creates, prioritizes, and refines user stories.
- Stakeholder Communication: Bridges the gap between business needs and the development team.
- Acceptance Criteria: Sets clear quality standards and reviews completed work.

Scrum Master's Core Responsibilities: 
- Removing Impediments: Clears daily blockers that slow the team down.
- Process Facilitation: Leads Scrum events like daily standups, planning, and retrospectives.
- Agile Coaching: Teaches self-management and continuous improvement.
- Protecting the Team: Shields developers from outside distractions and scope creep.

Q3. What are the 3 types of meetings that happen during a sprint cycle? List at least 2 points on what needs to be discussed during each of these meetings. 

Ans. 

1. Sprint Planning Meeting
- The team and the product owner discuss and define the main objective or business value the sprint should achieve. 
- The team picks specific tasks and user stories from the product backlog to move into the sprint backlog based on their capacity. 

2. Daily Stand-up (Daily Scrum) 
- Team members share what work was completed since the last meeting toward the sprint goal.
- Participants identify any obstacles or roadblocks getting in the way of their work so the team can help resolve them. 

3. Sprint Review Meeting
- The team showcases the new features, product increments, or bug fixes they built during the sprint.
- Stakeholders and product owners review the output, discuss what was delivered, and provide input that will shape the next steps.
